# Brand Core

## Executive Summary

Opus Archive is the definitive, text-first digital encyclopedia of classical music. Born from a frustration with superficial, ad-cluttered resources, it offers meticulously structured, academically rigorous articles in a distraction-free, elegant environment. It is the trusted, permanent home for those who seek not just to hear music, but to understand it deeply—from the architecture of a fugue to the historical context of a symphony. For scholars, professionals, and serious enthusiasts, Opus Archive is the essential, always-expanding monument to music’s intellectual and artistic heritage.

## Brand Story

The great musical encyclopedias of the past sit unopened on library shelves, while the web floods us with fragmented biographies, unreliable forum posts, and platforms where the music itself overshadows the knowledge behind it. Opus Archive began with a simple, ambitious conviction: that the profound world of classical music deserves a digital home as deep, serious, and beautifully structured as the art form itself. We set out to build a sanctuary for the written note, the analytical insight, and the historical detail—a place where a Brahms scholar and a curious undergraduate can stand on the same hallowed ground, immersed only in the pure pursuit of understanding. This is not a streaming service. It is a living, breathing archive for the mind.

## Vision & Mission

- **Vision:** To become the most authoritative, trusted, and comprehensive online knowledge resource for classical music worldwide—a modern-day monument to the intellect behind the art.
- **Mission:** To gather, rigorously verify, elegantly present, and continuously expand the world’s knowledge of classical music, adhering to the highest academic standards while delivering it through a serene, user-focused reading experience.

## Core Values

- **Uncompromising Scholarship:** Every article is deeply researched, meticulously cited, and written with academic integrity. We prioritize truth over speed.
- **Purity of Focus:** Music knowledge is the sole protagonist. We are proudly text-first, free from the distraction of embedded streams, downloads, or intrusive advertising.
- **Elegance in Structure:** Clarity is a form of beauty. Our information architecture, advanced tagging, and controlled vocabulary make the vastness of music history navigable and interconnected.
- **Permanent Authority:** We build for the long term. Like a great reference work, our content is stable, reliable, and treated as a living document that grows wiser with time.
- **Resonant Depth:** We don't stop at the surface. We explore the why, the how, the theoretical underpinnings, and the cultural reverberations of every work and composer.

## Messaging Pillars

- **The Deep Dive:** "Go beyond the composer’s dates. Discover the structural genius, the innovative harmony, the historical dialogue within every measure."
- **The Sanctuary of Text:** "In a world of noisy feeds and auto-play, here is a quiet, elegant space built solely for reading, learning, and deep thinking."
- **The Interconnected Web of Knowledge:** "See how a Gregorian chant connects to a Mahler symphony through our intelligently linked taxonomy—a research tool as powerful as the content itself."
- **The Definitive Standard:** "When a Wikipedia article isn’t enough and a scholarly database is too opaque, Opus Archive stands as the accessible, definitive middle ground for serious minds."

## Brand Positioning

For serious classical music students, scholars, and professional musicians, Opus Archive is the pure, scholarly knowledge platform that provides unmatched article depth and structural clarity. Unlike generalist encyclopedias, streaming-service blogs, or outdated print monographs, we offer a meticulously tagged, interconnected, and visually serene environment where the distraction-free pursuit of musical understanding is the sole mission.

## Brand Personality

Opus Archive is **The Erudite Custodian with a Modernist’s Heart.**

- **Authoritative but never pompous:** It speaks with the calm confidence of an expert, not the arrogance of a gatekeeper.
- **Meticulous yet inviting:** The detail is breathtaking, but the design and tone welcome the genuinely curious, not just the tenured professor.
- **Timeless yet contemporary:** It is rooted in a centuries-old tradition of scholarship, but expressed through a clean, minimal, and thoroughly modern design language. It wears its prestige with quiet elegance.

## Value Proposition

- **For the Graduate Student:** "A single, trusted source where you can trace the development of sonata form across three centuries, with every example logically indexed and annotated, saving you dozens of hours of scattered research."
- **For the Professional Performer:** "Deep program-note-level analysis that goes beyond anecdotes, giving you the intellectual ammunition to inform your interpretation and connect with your audience."
- **For the Educator:** "A distraction-free, authoritative resource to assign to students, knowing they will be immersed in meticulously structured knowledge, not clickbait."
- **Ultimate Promise:** "The world’s classical music knowledge, organized for the depths of your curiosity, presented with the elegance it deserves."

## Target Users & Brand Persona

- **The Graduate Musicology Researcher:** Needs citable, deeply footnoted articles with clear taxonomic links. Values the controlled vocabulary and the exhaustive index of works. Sees Opus Archive as an indispensable research tool.
- **The Conservatory Student:** Seeks to understand the theory behind the repertoire they are learning. Needs clear explanations of form, harmony, and historical context to enrich their performance. Comes for the Sonata Form article, stays for the interconnected web of ideas.
- **The Professional Orchestral Musician:** Prepares for a season and wants a sophisticated yet accessible deep-dive into a warhorse symphony. Craves the "Deep Dive" articles that reveal structural and timbral insights, going far beyond the typical program notes.
- **The Dedicated, Lifelong Amateur:** Loves classical music with a passion and wants to understand it on a technical level. Has been frustrated by the unreliability of Wikipedia and the shallowness of many music blogs. Finds in Opus Archive a dignified home for their intellectual curiosity.



# Verbal Brand

## Brand Name

**Opus Archive**

### Rationale

The name weds two pillars of classical music’s intellectual tradition. *Opus*—Latin for “work”—is the cataloguing heartbeat of the repertoire (Op. 1, Op. 131). It immediately signals the art form’s rigor, order, and creative legacy. *Archive* conveys permanence, trust, and comprehensive curation. Together, they promise a sanctuary where every musical work’s knowledge is preserved, organized, and honored.

### Pronunciation

- /ˈoʊ.pəs ˈɑːr.kaɪv/
- OH-puhss AR-kive

### Written Form

The name is always written as two separate words with initial capitals: **Opus Archive**. The definite article “The” is omitted in the official brand name for modern directness, but may appear in running prose (“the Opus Archive platform”). The name is never abbreviated, hyphenated, or combined into one word.

## Brand Voice & Tone

The voice of Opus Archive is that of **The Erudite Custodian with a Modernist’s Heart**—calm, precise, and illuminating. It speaks with the confidence of deep learning and the generosity of a true teacher.

### Voice Dimensions

- **Scholarly, not pedantic.**
  We use precise terminology and accurate musicological vocabulary, but we never lapse into jargon for its own sake. Clarity is our ultimate sophistication.
  - *Example:* “The development section fractures the theme into its motivic atoms, subjecting each to a series of remote modulations before a fragmented retransition.”
  - *Not:* “The development section engages in motivic liquidation across a variety of tonal regions.”

- **Elegant, not ornate.**
  Sentences are well-crafted and graceful, but never overwrought. We favor the controlled beauty of a well-placed semicolon over a parade of adjectives.

- **Authoritative, yet inviting.**
  Facts are stated with quiet assurance. Questions are welcomed. We never condescend. We assume intelligence in our reader and reward curiosity with depth.

- **Distraction-free, yet never dry.**
  We don’t shout. We don’t use hyperbole or exclamation marks. The intellectual excitement comes from the material itself, conveyed through crisp, insightful prose that lets the content shine.

### Tone Spectrum

Our voice remains consistent, but its tone shifts slightly depending on context.

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Encyclopedia Entry (Composer) | Definitive, balanced, historically grounded, respectful. |
| Deep Dive Analysis (Work) | Insightful, analytical, structurally lucid; reveals the “how” and “why” behind the notes. |
| Concept & Theory Article (Form, Harmony) | Pedagogical, systematic, crystal-clear; builds understanding step by step. |
| Historical Context Essay | Narrative, vivid yet objective; paints the cultural and biographical landscape. |
| Help & Onboarding (UI text, tooltips) | Clear, helpful, warm without being chatty. “Find related works” not “Check this out!” |

## Writing Rules

These rules ensure that every article, tooltip, and metadata field speaks with one coherent Opus Archive voice.

### 1. Terminology & Nomenclature

- **Works:** Use the full standardized title at first mention, followed by common nickname or catalogue number in parentheses.
  - *Example:* Symphony No. 9 in D minor, Op. 125 (“Choral”)
  - Subsequent mentions may use “the Ninth Symphony” or “the symphony”.
- **Keys:** Always spell out the tonic and mode: G minor, not Gm; C-sharp minor, not C#m.
- **Composer Names:** Use the most familiar standard English form.
  - *Example:* Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, not Mozart, Wolfgang Amadeus.
  - For non-Western names, follow established academic convention.
- **Dates:** Provide composer dates at first mention: Ludwig van Beethoven (1770–1827). Use an en dash (–) for date ranges, not a hyphen.
- **Tempo & Expression Marks:** Retain original Italian/French/German, followed by English translation in parentheses on first use in an article, unless the term is so common it needs no translation (e.g., allegro, adagio).
  - *Example:* Andante con moto (at a walking pace, with motion)
  - A tempo giusto may be left untranslated in a specialist analysis.

### 2. Style & Grammar

- **Language:** British English spelling (colour, analyse, catalogue), except for proper nouns.
- **Citations:** Use a consistent academic style, preferably Chicago Manual of Style (Notes and Bibliography) for humanities content. Footnotes, not inline parentheticals.
- **Quotations:** Use single quotation marks for direct speech or cited text, double quotation marks for quotes within quotes.
- **Numerals:** Spell out numbers one through ninety-nine, use numerals for 100 and above, except at the beginning of a sentence. Opus numbers and catalogue numbers are always numerals (Op. 131, K. 551).
- **Italics:** Italicise titles of major works (symphonies, operas, tone poems, ballets); use roman with quotation marks for songs and short pieces.
  - *Example:* *Die Zauberflöte* (opera)
  - “Erlkönig” (song)
  - *Symphonie fantastique*
- **Composers’ Words:** When quoting letters or writings, retain original spelling and punctuation, with translations provided if in a foreign language.
- **Neutral Point of View:** Describe, analyse, and contextualise; never editorialise or offer personal opinion. Avoid words like “greatest”, “masterpiece”, “unfortunately”. Let the evidence and analysis convey significance.
- **Sentence Structure:** Favor clear, medium-length sentences. Alternate for rhythm, but ensure each sentence carries one clear idea. Avoid excessively long subordinate clauses that obscure meaning.

### 3. Structural & Metadata Rules

- **Article Structure:**
  - Lead section: a concise, standalone overview (50–80 words) defining the subject.
  - Body: logical sections with clear headings (Biography, Works, Style, Legacy).
  - Infobox (where applicable): standardised key facts (dates, catalogue numbers, key works).
- **Controlled Vocabulary:** Adhere strictly to the project’s established tags and taxonomies. Authors must tag each article with the appropriate period, form, key, instrumentation, and relevant concepts.
- **Internal Linking:** Link to other Opus Archive articles on first significant mention of a related term, composer, or work. Avoid overlinking; link only when the connection is meaningful for deeper inquiry.
- **Images & Media Captions:** Captions should be informative, contextual, and written in present tense for the image content. “A portrait of Clara Schumann by Franz von Lenbach, 1878.”
- **Alt Text:** Provide concise, descriptive alt text for all images, suitable for screen readers, focusing on the visual content’s informative value.

### 4. Tone Enforcement Checklist

Before publishing, authors and editors should ask:

- Is the language precise and musicologically sound?
- Could a knowledgeable amateur follow this without feeling lost?
- Are statements of fact supported by citation where appropriate?
- Is the prose elegant and uncluttered, free of unnecessary adjectives?
- Does the article feel inviting, or does it intimidate?
- Have I avoided hyperbole, editorializing, and value judgments?
- Is the metadata (tags, catalog numbers, dates) accurate and complete?



# Visual Brand

## Logo System

The Opus Archive logo is a wordmark—unadorned, precise, and timeless. It eschews pictorial symbols in favour of pure typographic dignity.

### Primary Logo

- **Construction:** The name “Opus Archive” set on a single line, with a subtle structural detail that alludes to order and cataloguing: a fine, 1pt vertical rule (or an identical dot) in the brand’s accent colour, separating the two words.
- **Arrangement:** Opus (light weight) | Archive (regular/book weight). The rule bridges the gap, functioning like a catalogue divider.
- **Alternative Concept:** Two-line stacked arrangement for square/vertical contexts: Opus centred above Archive, rendered in a single weight, with an elegantly generous line-height. The rule disappears, replaced by simple spatial tension.

### Clear Space & Minimum Sizes

- **Clear Space:** The height of the capital “O” on all four sides of the logo lockup. This isolation preserves its gravity.
- **Minimum Size:**
  - Print: 40mm wide.
  - Digital: 120px wide for the primary horizontal lockup; 90px for the stacked variant.

### Colour Variations

| Variant | Usage |
|---|---|
| Full Colour | Default. “Opus Archive” in Ink (#1C1C1C), with the separating rule/dot in Burnished Gold (#BFA054). |
| Reversed (Light) | White (#FFFFFF) wordmark with Gold accent rule on dark backgrounds (Ink, photography, or dark mode). |
| Monochrome (Ink) | All elements in Ink, for single-colour applications where gold is unavailable. |
| Monochrome (White) | All elements in White, for dark, single-colour backgrounds. |

### Unacceptable Treatments

- Never stretch, compress, or skew the wordmark.
- Never add drop shadows, gradients, or outlines.
- Never separate the two words of the logo without the dividing rule (except in the stacked variant).
- Never use the logo in any colour other than the approved palette.
- Never place the logo on a busy, low-contrast background.

## Typography

The type system marries the scholarly gravitas of a fine serif with the lucid neutrality of a contemporary sans-serif.

### Primary Typeface (Headings & Brand): Cormorant Garamond

- **Rationale:** Designed by Christian Thalmann, it draws on the legacy of Claude Garamond’s Renaissance types, evoking the era of early music printing while offering a crisp, modern digital finish. Its generous contrast and elegant italics convey history without nostalgia.
- **Weights Used:** Light (for logo), Regular (body headings), Medium (major section titles), SemiBold Italic (accent words, subheadings), Bold (infobox titles).
- **Fallback Stack:** Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif

### Secondary Typeface (Body & UI): Inter

- **Rationale:** Inter is built for screens, with a tall x-height and open apertures that ensure legibility in long-form reading. Its neutral, clean character recedes gracefully, letting the content dominate.
- **Weights Used:** Regular (body text, 400), Medium (UI labels, small emphasis, 500), SemiBold (buttons, navigation, 600).
- **Fallback Stack:** 'Helvetica Neue', Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif

### Typographic Scale (Digital)

## Image & Photography Style

Opus Archive is text-first. Images are used only when they deepen understanding, not to decorate. Every image is treated with the same scholarly reverence as the text.

### Tone & Subjects

- **Archival & Documentary:** Historical portraits of composers, photographs of original manuscripts, architectural drawings of concert halls, period instrument photographs, and musicological diagrams.
- **Mood:** Contemplative, atmospheric but never sentimental. Light feels natural and directional—like reading by a tall window in a nineteenth-century library.
